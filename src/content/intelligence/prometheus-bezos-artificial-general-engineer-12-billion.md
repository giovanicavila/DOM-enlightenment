Prometheus, the physical AI startup co-founded by Jeff Bezos and former Verily co-founder Vik Bajaj, announced on June 11, 2026 that it raised **$12 billion** at a **$41 billion valuation** from investors including JPMorgan Chase, Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, DST Global, and Arch Venture Partners. Combined with its initial $6.2 billion raise in late 2025, the company has now raised more than $18 billion — making it one of the best-funded AI startups ever, despite only 150 employees across offices in San Francisco, London, and Zurich.

Prometheus is building what Bezos calls an **"artificial general engineer"** — AI software designed to automate the design and manufacturing of complex physical systems, from jet engines and aerospace components to pharmaceutical compounds and computer hardware. The goal is to dramatically compress the invention-to-product cycle that underpins industrial progress. Unlike the current wave of AI companies focused on chatbots, text generation, and software automation, Prometheus targets the physical economy — applying advanced machine learning to computer-aided design, materials science, and manufacturing simulation.

Bezos told CNBC that AI-driven productivity gains would create what he calls "labor scarcity" rather than unemployment — a world where demand for human workers outpaces supply as AI raises the standard of living. He argued that "significant productivity in the economy is going to raise the standard of living," enabling two-earner households to become one-earner households and reducing overtime. The company has not disclosed specific technical details about its current capabilities, but Bezos described what Prometheus has already built as "really quite remarkable."

**Why it matters:** Prometheus represents the most ambitious bet yet on AI for the physical world — and at $18 billion in total funding with Bezos personally at the helm, it signals that the next frontier of AI competition extends beyond software into the design and manufacturing of everything we build.
