NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang took the stage at Computex 2026 in Taipei on June 1 to announce RTX Spark, a revolutionary Arm-based superchip that fuses a 20-core NVIDIA Grace CPU with a Blackwell GPU packing 6,144 CUDA cores and fifth-generation Tensor Cores. The chip uses unified LPDDR5X memory configurable up to 128GB, enabling approximately 1 petaflop of AI compute — enough to run 120-billion-parameter language models entirely on-device with a one-million-token context window.

Built in partnership with MediaTek for the CPU side and Microsoft for Windows integration, RTX Spark represents NVIDIA's first full silicon solution for the PC market. Huang declared, "40 years later, Microsoft and NVIDIA are going to reinvent the PC." The chip is designed for what NVIDIA calls the "agentic AI OS" era, where local AI agents handle long-running tasks without cloud dependency. Adobe is already rebuilding parts of Premiere and Photoshop to tap into Spark's unified memory architecture, promising up to 2x faster AI features.

Microsoft unveiled the Surface Laptop Ultra as the flagship RTX Spark device, alongside systems from ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, and MSI. First shipments are expected between September and November 2026. The chip also supports 1440p AAA gaming at 100+ fps with ray tracing, positioning it as a do-everything platform for creators, developers, and gamers.

**Why it matters:** RTX Spark breaks Intel and AMD's long-standing duopoly on Windows PC processors and, for the first time, brings data-center-grade AI inference to thin-and-light laptops, potentially making local LLM inference as ubiquitous as GPU-accelerated graphics.
