NVIDIA made two major AI model announcements at its Computex 2026 keynote in Taipei on June 1. **Nemotron 3 Ultra**, NVIDIA's largest open-weight language model, features approximately 550 billion total parameters in a sparse Mixture-of-Experts architecture with only 55 billion active per token. It supports a 1 million token context window and is specifically optimized for long-running, multi-step agentic AI workloads — planning, tool use, orchestration, and error recovery across extended sessions. A pre-release endpoint on DeepInfra served over 300 tokens per second.

**Cosmos 3** is a fundamentally different kind of model: the world's first fully open omnimodel for Physical AI. Built on a breakthrough mixture-of-transformers architecture pairing a reasoning transformer with an expert diffusion generator, it natively understands and generates text, images, video, ambient sound, and action trajectories within a single system. Cosmos 3 tops open-weight leaderboards in both text-to-image and image-to-video benchmarks, and is designed to reduce physical AI training and evaluation cycles from months to days.

NVIDIA also launched the **Cosmos Coalition**, bringing together Agile Robots, Black Forest Labs, Runway, Skild AI, and others to advance open world models for robotics and autonomous vehicles. Early adopters include Doosan Robotics, LG Electronics, Samsung, and Li Auto. Both models are fully open-weight under the OpenMDW 1.1 license via Hugging Face and NVIDIA's developer portal.

**Why it matters:** NVIDIA is extending its AI strategy from hardware into foundational models themselves — offering developers open-weight alternatives for both advanced language reasoning and physical AI, creating a full-stack ecosystem that runs on NVIDIA hardware while giving enterprises full customization and data sovereignty.
