NVIDIA announced the Isaac GR00T Reference Humanoid Robot at GTC Taipei on June 1, 2026 — the industry's first open, fully integrated humanoid robot reference design. Built on the Isaac GR00T development platform, it combines a Unitree H2 Plus humanoid chassis, Sharpa Wave tactile five-finger hands for dexterous manipulation, and an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor T5000 compute module delivering 2,070 FP4 teraflops of AI performance from a chip consuming as little as 40 watts.

The reference design unifies what has historically been a fragmented process spanning hardware integration, data collection, simulation, training, and deployment. The open software stack — available on GitHub — includes everything needed to collect demonstration data, train robot foundation models, simulate behavior in virtual environments, and deploy trained policies onto physical hardware. Leading research institutions including Ai2, ETH Zurich, Stanford Robotics Center, and UC San Diego's Advanced Robotics and Controls Laboratory are already using the platform.

Jensen Huang framed the announcement as opening a "multitrillion-dollar economic opportunity," as humanoid robots transition from prototypes to scaled commercial deployments. Figure AI has achieved 1 robot per hour production at its BotQ factory, and Agility Digit has 7+ active units at Toyota Canada. NVIDIA Halos, announced concurrently, provides the first full-stack functional safety system for physical AI — bringing autonomous-vehicle-grade safety certification to humanoids and industrial robots.

**Why it matters:** By open-sourcing the brain and standardizing the body of a humanoid robot, NVIDIA is doing for physical AI what Android did for smartphones — creating a shared, reproducible platform that could collapse the development cycle for general-purpose robotics from years to months.
