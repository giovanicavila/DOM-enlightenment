OpenAI and Broadcom today unveiled **Jalapeño**, OpenAI's first custom Intelligence Processor — an accelerator architected from the ground up for LLM inference. Developed from initial design to manufacturing tape-out in just nine months, the chip represents what the companies believe to be the fastest ASIC development cycle ever achieved in high-performance semiconductors.

Jalapeño was co-designed using OpenAI's own models to accelerate parts of the design and optimization process. Early testing shows performance per watt "substantially better" than current state-of-the-art inference hardware, achieved through an architecture that reduces data movement and balances compute, memory, and networking resources to reach much closer to theoretical peak utilization. Broadcom's Tomahawk networking silicon helps bring the platform to large-scale production.

The chip is the first step in a multi-generation compute platform designed for initial deployment by the end of 2026. OpenAI is partnering with Celestica for board, rack, and system-level integration, and plans to deploy Jalapeño at gigawatt scale with data center partners over successive generations. The announcement comes alongside OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol preview, and positions the company as a full-stack AI platform — from products to models to silicon.

Why it matters: Custom inference silicon gives OpenAI vertical integration, reducing dependence on NVIDIA GPUs and potentially lowering inference costs dramatically — a strategic move that could reshape the economics of serving frontier models at scale.
