OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled **Jalapeño** on June 24, 2026 — OpenAI's first custom "Intelligence Processor" purpose-built for large language model inference. The chip marks OpenAI's entry into semiconductor design, joining Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium/Inferentia), and Microsoft as hyperscale AI players developing custom silicon to reduce dependence on NVIDIA GPUs.

Jalapeño is an application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) designed from the ground up for LLM inference workloads — the production-stage compute that powers every ChatGPT, Codex, and API response. OpenAI claims early tests show "substantially better performance per watt" than current state-of-the-art alternatives, though final benchmarks and a detailed technical report are still pending. The chip went from initial design to manufacturing tape-out in just nine months, which OpenAI says is the fastest ASIC development cycle for a high-performance semiconductor, accelerated by OpenAI's own models used during the design process.

Broadcom handled silicon implementation and contributed Tomahawk networking technology, while Celestica manages board, rack, and system-level integration. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan confirmed plans to deploy Jalapeño at gigawatt scale with Microsoft and other partners starting in late 2026. Microsoft is expected to purchase approximately 40% of the chips. The Jalapeño announcement follows OpenAI's broader October 2025 partnership with Broadcom to build a multi-generation compute platform, including a planned 10-gigawatt infrastructure buildout by 2029.

**Why it matters:** Custom inference silicon could fundamentally shift the economics of running AI at scale — if Jalapeño delivers on its performance-per-watt claims, it reduces OpenAI's dependency on NVIDIA's GPU ecosystem and lowers the marginal cost of every ChatGPT interaction, making AI more affordable and accessible.
