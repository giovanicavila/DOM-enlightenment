IBM unveiled the world's first sub-1 nanometer chip technology on June 25, 2026, marking a landmark breakthrough for an industry long thought to be approaching the physical limits of silicon scaling. Built around a revolutionary three-dimensional "nanostack" transistor architecture, the 0.7 nm (7 angstrom) node packs nearly 100 billion transistors onto a die the size of a fingernail — roughly twice the density of IBM's 2 nm chip from 2021.

The nanostack architecture represents a fundamental departure from conventional chip design. Instead of laying transistors flat on a single plane, IBM researchers developed a method to vertically stack and stagger transistors using 3D sequential integration. This unlocks the ability to use different material combinations within each stacked layer, optimizing each transistor's performance and power efficiency independently. IBM experimentally validated the architecture through ultra-thin dielectric bonding in CMOS integration, dual-channel engineering, and functional CMOS inverter operation — confirming the technology can be physically built and supports real computation.

The performance gains are substantial: up to 50% more performance or 70% greater energy efficiency compared to IBM's 2 nm node chips. At the VLSI 2026 conference, IBM also demonstrated that the nanostack architecture provides 40% scaling in SRAM, enabling chip designers to create much more efficient chips while supporting the high-bandwidth data demands of advanced AI workloads. IBM projects a path to production within the next five years, with the technology expected to sustain semiconductor scaling for at least another decade.

The breakthrough was achieved at IBM's research facility in Albany, New York, which will soon house a High NA EUV lithography tool essential for future logic scaling. IBM also recently announced plans to form Anderon, the world's first pure-play quantum foundry.

**Why it matters:** This breakthrough extends Moore's Law into the angstrom era at a time when many had declared traditional scaling dead. For AI, sub-1nm chips mean dramatically more compute per watt — directly enabling larger models, longer training runs, and more efficient inference at the frontier.
