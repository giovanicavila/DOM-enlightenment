Google DeepMind suffered an unprecedented talent exodus in the third week of June 2026, losing four senior AI researchers to Anthropic and one to OpenAI within days. The departures triggered a sell-off that wiped more than $270 billion from Alphabet's market capitalization.

The highest-profile exit was **John Jumper**, the Nobel Prize-winning co-creator of AlphaFold, who announced his departure on June 19 after nearly nine years at DeepMind. Days earlier, **Noam Shazeer** — a co-author of the seminal 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" paper and co-lead of the Gemini model — left for OpenAI. By June 24, Bloomberg reported that **Jonas Adler** and **Alexander Pritzel**, both key contributors to Gemini's development, were heading to Anthropic. **Arthur Conmy**, a safety researcher who had worked on Gemini 2.5's safeguards, also joined Anthropic. In total, four of the five went to Anthropic, the AI startup in which Google itself has invested up to $10 billion.

The timing compounds the damage: Gemini 3.5 Pro was subsequently delayed to July for "final adjustments," with industry observers linking the delay to the sudden loss of engineering leadership. DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis publicly downplayed the departures, noting the company's workforce has grown 5% year-over-year to approximately 194,700 employees, but analysts pointed out that DeepMind engineers are now 11 times more likely to leave for Anthropic than the reverse.

The exodus highlights a larger shift in the AI talent market, where elite researchers increasingly favor startups and smaller labs over Big Tech. Anthropic has positioned itself as the primary beneficiary, using its ~$350 billion valuation and Google's own investment to build a formidable research bench.

**Why it matters:** The departure of a Nobel laureate and multiple Gemini leads in a single week represents a catastrophic talent bleed for Google at a critical moment in the frontier AI race, and signals that Anthropic has become the premier destination for top AI research talent.
