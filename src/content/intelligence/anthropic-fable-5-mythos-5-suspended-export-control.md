On June 12, 2026, Anthropic received an export control directive from US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick citing national security authorities, ordering the company to suspend all access to Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 by any foreign national — a restriction that effectively forced Anthropic to disable both models for every customer globally, including US citizens. The directive arrived at 5:21 PM ET, giving the company no advance notice.

The trigger was a claimed "jailbreak" demonstration by another company that allegedly bypassed Mythos 5's safeguards. Anthropic reviewed the technique and characterized it as a "narrow, non-universal jailbreak" that revealed minor vulnerabilities also present in other frontier models, including OpenAI's GPT-5.5. Anthropic publicly stated it disagreed with the recall, called it a "misunderstanding," and noted it was complying while working to restore access. The models had launched only three days earlier on June 9, making this the shortest lifespan of any frontier AI deployment.

This marks the first time the US government has used export control authority (the Export Controls Reform Act of 2018) to force a recall of a commercial AI model. All other Anthropic models — including Claude Opus 4.8, Sonnet 4.6, and Haiku 4.5 — remain fully available. President Trump later signaled in a June 19 Axios interview that he no longer views Anthropic as a security threat, though as of June 27 both models remain suspended.

**Why it matters:** This sets a watershed precedent for government intervention in AI deployment, establishing that frontier models can be pulled offline via national security authorities, and underscores the operational risk of single-model dependence for enterprises building on cutting-edge AI.
