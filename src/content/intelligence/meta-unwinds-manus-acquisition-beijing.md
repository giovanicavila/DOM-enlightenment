China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) ordered Meta to unwind its $2 billion acquisition of Manus, the Singapore-based AI agent startup that went viral for its autonomous web agents. The NDRC launched a probe in early 2026 citing national security and technology export control concerns, and by April had ordered the deal reversed. Meta spent the following months disentangling Manus from its internal systems, with full separation underway by mid-June.

Manus was founded in 2022 by Chinese entrepreneurs and relocated from Beijing to Singapore in 2024, eventually winding down its China operations and swapping Chinese investors for US venture capital — a restructuring regulators referred to as "Singapore-washing," the same term applied to TikTok's corporate reorganization. Despite these moves, Beijing maintained that the technology was developed in China and thus subject to its export controls. Manus's co-founders are now reportedly raising approximately $1 billion from outside investors to buy back the startup, with a potential Hong Kong listing as an end goal.

The unwinding comes in the same week the US government restricted access to Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models, creating a striking symmetry: the US fences capability in (keeping frontier models from foreign nationals), while China fences talent in (preventing AI teams from being acquired by US companies). Both moves signal that AI assets are increasingly treated as strategic national resources rather than ordinary commercial property.

Why it matters: The forced unwinding of an already-closed $2 billion acquisition on national security grounds establishes a precedent that will reshape every cross-border AI deal — talent, code, and algorithmic IP developed in China may now effectively be off-limits to US buyers regardless of corporate restructuring.
