OpenAI began a limited preview of GPT-5.6 on June 26, 2026, introducing a radical three-tier model family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — under a new naming system that decouples capability tiers from model generations. Sol is the flagship, posting a state-of-the-art 91.9% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, beating Anthropic's Mythos 5 (84.3%) and Fable 5 (83.4%) on agentic command-line coding. Terra delivers GPT-5.5-class performance at half the price ($2.50/$15 per 1M tokens), and Luna is OpenAI's most affordable model ever at $1/$6 per 1M tokens.

The launch introduces two new reasoning modes: `max`, which gives Sol deeper thinking time, and `ultra`, which goes beyond a single agent by orchestrating sub-agents for complex multi-step work. OpenAI dedicated over 700,000 A100-equivalent GPU hours to automated red-teaming, calling GPT-5.6 its "most robust safety stack to date." All three models hit the "High" cybersecurity threshold under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework — a first for an entire product line.

For the first time, the U.S. government preemptively restricted a frontier model's release. The White House requested that GPT-5.6 launch be limited to roughly 20 government-vetted organizations, citing national security concerns over the model's "Mythos-like" capability. OpenAI complied but pushed back publicly, stating: "We don't believe this kind of government access process should become the long-term default. It keeps the best tools from users, developers, enterprises, cyber defenders, and global partners who need them."

**Why it matters:** The GPT-5.6 launch establishes a precedent for government-gated frontier AI releases, creates a durable three-tier pricing structure that could reshape enterprise AI procurement, and signals that the model capability race has entered a phase where national security controls, not just technical benchmarks, determine who gets access to the most powerful systems.
