OpenAI today announced the GPT-5.6 model family, introducing three capability tiers under a new naming convention. **Sol** is the flagship model for the hardest problems — complex coding, biology research, and cybersecurity defense. **Terra** delivers competitive performance to GPT-5.5 at half the cost, targeting everyday enterprise workloads. **Luna** is the fastest and most affordable tier, designed for high-volume tasks like summarization and drafting.

The release introduces a "max" reasoning effort setting and an "ultra" multi-agent mode that spawns sub-agents for complex, multi-step work. OpenAI says Sol features its most robust safety stack yet, with strengthened protections for high-risk activity, sensitive cyber requests, and repeated misuse, hardened through weeks of automated red-teaming.

However, the launch is unusually restricted. At the request of the Trump administration — following a June 2 executive order on AI safety — OpenAI is limiting initial access to approximately 20 trusted partner organizations. The company pushed back against the precedent, stating in its announcement: "We don't believe this kind of government access process should become the long-term default." API pricing ranges from $1–$30 per million input tokens depending on the tier.

Why it matters: GPT-5.6 Sol marks a genuine leap in agentic capability, but the government-coordinated preview signals a new era of frontier model release controls that could reshape how and when cutting-edge AI reaches the public.
