NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang unveiled **Nemotron 3 Ultra** at Computex 2026 in Taipei on June 1, the largest and most capable open-weights model ever released by a US organization. The model packs approximately 550 billion parameters using a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture, with roughly 55 billion parameters active per token — delivering over 300 output tokens per second and costing roughly 30% less to run than comparable frontier models.

Nemotron 3 Ultra ranks 48 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of all other US open-weights models including Google's Gemma 4 31B (39), NVIDIA's own Nemotron 3 Super (36), and OpenAI's gpt-oss-120b (33). However, it still trails Chinese open-source leaders like Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.6 (54), underscoring the competitive gap in the open-weights race between US and Chinese labs. The model ships on Hugging Face and NVIDIA NIM under a permissive open-weights license, enabling enterprises to fine-tune, self-host, and customize the model without API dependency.

Huang also announced the **NVIDIA Vera CPU**, a purpose-built processor for agentic AI and reinforcement learning workloads, claiming twice the efficiency and 50% faster performance than traditional x86 server CPUs. Early adopters include OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX — the three companies most aggressively scaling agentic AI deployments. Together, the Nemotron 3 Ultra and Vera CPU announcements signal NVIDIA's transformation from a chip supplier into a full-stack AI platform company that competes with its own customers in models and infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** Nemotron 3 Ultra gives enterprises a frontier-grade open-weights alternative to proprietary models for the first time — potentially reshaping the build-vs-buy calculus for any organization deploying AI agents at scale.
