Apple used its June 8 WWDC 2026 keynote — Tim Cook's last as CEO — to execute a full-scale AI reset. The centerpiece: Siri has been rebuilt from the ground up using a licensed, custom Google Gemini model wrapped in Apple's privacy architecture. The new Siri gains a standalone app across iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, and Vision Pro, multi-step reasoning (e.g., "Find the World Cup schedule, plan a viewing party, and recommend dishes from both countries' cuisines"), personal context awareness across messages, calendars, and photos, and a deep research voice mode comparable to ChatGPT and Gemini Live.

Apple emphasized that all data is processed under its Private Cloud Compute architecture, with on-device Apple Foundation Models handling what they can and cloud inference occurring in privacy-enforced enclaves that external auditors can verify. Notably, Siri AI will not launch in the European Union at release, signaling unresolved tensions between Apple's AI architecture and EU regulatory requirements.

Alongside Siri, Apple announced iOS 27 (supporting iPhone 11 and newer, with 70% faster photo loading and 80% faster AirDrop), macOS Golden Gate (Apple Silicon-only, with a new opacity slider for the Liquid Glass design system), and Apple Intelligence 2.0 features including photorealistic Image Playground, three new AI photo tools (Clean Up, Extend, Spatial Reframing), natural-language Shortcuts automation, and system-wide Visual Intelligence.

**Why it matters:** Apple's decision to partner with Google — its primary search rival — for the intelligence layer of its most personal feature marks a historic shift in the AI landscape, effectively locking in Gemini as the default AI engine on over 2 billion devices and reshaping the competitive dynamics between the Google-Microsoft-OpenAI-Anthropic ecosystem.
