OpenAI launched **GPT-5.5-Cyber**, a specialized variant of its flagship model fine-tuned for cybersecurity tasks, alongside the **"Patch the Planet"** initiative — a program that uses AI to automatically identify and fix vulnerabilities in critical open-source software. The announcement marks OpenAI's most direct entry into the AI-for-security space, competing with specialized offerings from Anthropic's Project Glasswing and Google's security AI efforts.

GPT-5.5-Cyber is designed for vulnerability discovery, exploit analysis, and secure code generation. Early testing shows the model can identify classes of vulnerabilities — including SQL injection, buffer overflows, and cross-site scripting — in large codebases with higher precision than general-purpose models. The "Patch the Planet" initiative operationalizes this capability by running automated scans against widely-used open-source dependencies and submitting verified patches to maintainers, potentially addressing the chronic security debt in the open-source ecosystem.

The launch arrives amid escalating competition in AI security: Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5, released just two weeks earlier, was specifically restricted by the US government due to its cybersecurity capabilities, while Google has been expanding its own AI-powered security tooling through Gemini. OpenAI's approach differs by focusing on the open-source ecosystem and making the patches publicly available, positioning GPT-5.5-Cyber as a defensive tool rather than the dual-use weapon that triggered Mythos 5's restriction.

Why it matters: The AI security arms race is accelerating — as frontier models grow more capable of finding vulnerabilities, the distinction between offensive and defensive applications becomes the central policy question, and initiatives like "Patch the Planet" represent a constructive answer to the dual-use dilemma.
