Anthropic's most advanced AI models, **Claude Mythos 5** and **Claude Fable 5**, became the center of a landmark government intervention in AI on June 12, 2026, when the Trump administration effectively banned their use by foreign nationals over national security concerns — reportedly after a group linked to China accessed Mythos. Anthropic responded by taking both models offline worldwide. Two weeks later, on June 26, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick authorized Anthropic to redeploy Mythos 5 to more than 100 specific U.S. government agencies and companies, including allowing non-American employees at those organizations to access the model.

The incident is unprecedented in AI industry history. The administration cited Mythos's advanced cybersecurity capabilities — Rep. Andrew Garbarino (R-NY), chair of the House Homeland Security Committee, emerged from a closed-door demonstration saying the model could identify vulnerabilities in banking systems and withdraw money from accounts. Anthropic's Project Glasswing, launched in April 2026, had already restricted access to select U.S. banks and tech companies including JPMorgan Chase, Amazon, and Microsoft. Notably, Fable 5 — the version Mythos 5 was based on, which was briefly released publicly — remains blocked, and Anthropic continues working with the government to restore general availability.

**Why it matters:** The Mythos ban and partial release establish a de facto government vetting process for frontier AI models, raising fundamental questions about how AI access will be governed — whether through temporary executive decisions or the comprehensive federal AI framework proposed in the Great American AI Act.
